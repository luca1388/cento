Birthday App Naming

Five name candidates with a logo mark, an app icon, a palette and a tagline in Italian and English. All names are Italian words that an English speaker can read and say, since the app is Italian-first and bilingual. The first one is the recommendation.

Candidates

Each mark is designed to work at 20 px in a widget header and at 1024 px as a store icon, with no gradients and one or two colors.

cento
Recommended
Cento means "one hundred". It comes from the Italian birthday wish cento di questi giorni, "a hundred of these days". Two syllables, no accents, nothing to explain to an English speaker who sees the "100" in the mark.
Cento di questi giorni.A hundred more of these days.
Dark and light icon variants
Mark
A candle as the "1" and two rings as the zeros. The flame is the only warm element.
Type
Fraunces, a soft serif, lowercase. Feels like a greeting card rather than a utility.
Palette
Midnight blue and candlelight cream, flame orange as the accent, raspberry for "today".
Watch out
"Cento" is a common word, so expect name collisions in store searches. Pair it with a qualifier in the store listing, such as "Cento: compleanni".
Soffio
Runner-up
Soffio is "a breath" or "a puff", the moment you blow out the candles. Playful and physical. English speakers read it as "SOF-fee-oh" without trouble.
Un soffio e ci sei.One breath, and you're there.
Teal and coral icon variants
Mark
A flame leaning away from three strokes of air. Reads as motion even at widget size.
Type
Bricolage Grotesque, heavy, capital S. Friendly and modern.
Palette
Deep teal with coral flame. Unusual for the category, which is mostly pink and purple.
Watch out
The word has no meaning in English, so the icon has to carry the idea.
candela
Candela is "candle", and also the SI unit of light. It is the safest name: obvious, warm, and already a near-English word.
Nessun compleanno al buio.No birthday in the dark.
Raspberry and night icon variants
Mark
One flame, a dot for the wick. The flame alone is the favicon and widget glyph.
Type
Sora, geometric and tight. Lowercase to stay soft.
Palette
Raspberry and plum, with a warm amber for the flame in dark mode.
Watch out
Most crowded name of the five. Several "Candela" apps exist in unrelated categories, so check trademarks early.
Auguri
Auguri is what Italians say on a birthday: "best wishes". It names the outcome of the app, the message you did not forget to send. Strongly Italian in flavor.
Mai più un "scusa, in ritardo".Never another "sorry, I'm late".
Violet and lime icon variants
Mark
A speech bubble holding three pieces of confetti. The bubble ties it to the "message" idea.
Type
Bricolage Grotesque, heavy. Loud, like the word itself.
Palette
Electric violet and lime, the most energetic of the set.
Watch out
Needs explaining outside Italy. Fine if the English market is secondary.
Festa
Festa is "party". Known to English speakers through "fiesta" and "festival". The most generic name here, and the one that says the least about reminders.
La festa comincia prima.The party starts earlier.
White and night icon variants
Mark
Four confetti shapes in four colors. Cheerful, but it is also what many party apps already use.
Type
Fraunces italic, to add some character to a plain word.
Palette
Multi-color on white. Hard to keep consistent across a whole UI.
Watch out
Very hard to find in store search and almost certainly taken as a domain and trademark.

Other names considered

Shorter notes on names that did not make the board, in case one of them resonates.

Evviva"Hooray". Fun, but hard for English speakers to spell after hearing it.
Ricorda"Remember". Describes the feature, not the feeling. Reads like a to-do app.
TantiFrom "tanti auguri". Short and cute, but meaningless alone.
Annata"Vintage", as in wine years. Clever for ages, a bit cold for birthdays.
Torta"Cake". Obvious icon, but it is a food word first and a birthday word second.
Fiammella"Little flame". Lovely in Italian, four syllables too many in English.

Before you commit to a name

  1. Search the App Store and Google Play for the exact name and the name plus "compleanni" or "birthday". A name is usable if yours can rank for its own exact match.
  2. Check the .app and .it domains, plus the handle on the social networks you plan to use. Consistency matters more than owning the .com.
  3. Run a trademark search on EUIPO and USPTO in class 9 (software) and class 42 (online services). A plain Italian word is weak as a trademark, so a distinctive mark and a tagline carry the brand.
  4. Reserve the bundle identifier early, for example it.yourdomain.cento, since it cannot change after the first store release.
  5. Say the name out loud in an English sentence: "Did you add her to Cento?" If it sounds natural, it travels.
